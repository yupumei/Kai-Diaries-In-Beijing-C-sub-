## 🍜 A Little Taste of Chinese Culture

Here are a few examples of the cultural details explored in this project.

### 1. Douzhi (豆汁儿) vs. Soy Milk (豆浆)

Although their Chinese names look similar, **Douzhi and soy milk are completely different drinks.**

Soy milk is made from soybeans and can be served with sweet or savory seasonings.

Beijing's traditional Douzhi, on the other hand, is a fermented drink made from the liquid produced during the processing of mung bean starch. It has a distinctive sour taste and smell.

Understanding this difference helps international viewers appreciate why Douzhi can be such a surprising experience for first-time drinkers!

### 2. The Chinese Expression “色香味俱全”

This four-character expression describes food that excels in three aspects:

- **色 — Appearance and color**
- **香 — Aroma**
- **味 — Taste**

In the program, Kai and Yang PD explore food by looking at it, smelling it, and tasting it. The production team connects these actions to the individual characters in this expression.

It's a lovely example of how Chinese language and food culture can come together in a playful way.

### 3. The Difference Between “二” (èr) and “两” (liǎng)

Both expressions are related to the number two, but they are used differently in Chinese.

For example, “两” is commonly used before measure words when counting things, while “二” appears in contexts such as counting numbers and reading certain numerical expressions.

Kai's efforts to learn Chinese and consciously correct his mistakes are part of what makes these moments so charming. Language learning is not only about getting everything right — it's also about noticing the differences and trying again!

### 4. Beijing's Traditional Food and Local Streets

The program introduces viewers to local Beijing food and the city's distinctive street culture.

Some examples include:

- **卤煮火烧 (Lǔzhǔ Huǒshāo):** A traditional Beijing dish made with pork offal, tofu, and baked wheat-flour bread simmered in seasoned broth.
- **炸酱面 (Zhájiàng Miàn):** Beijing-style noodles served with a savory fermented soybean paste sauce.
- **驴打滚 (Lǘdǎgǔn):** A traditional sweet made from glutinous rice and red bean paste, coated in soybean flour.
- **烟袋斜街 (Yāndài Xiéjiē):** A historic Beijing street near Shichahai, known for its traditional atmosphere and distinctive shops.

These details help viewers discover the cultural background behind the places Kai visits and the food he tries.

### 5. Chinese Music, Lyrics, and Internet Culture

Some moments in the program involve Chinese songs, colloquial expressions, or internet memes.

A literal translation may not always communicate the original meaning or humor. Where relevant, this project provides additional explanations to help translators understand the references and recreate their meaning naturally in another language.

The goal is not to translate every word mechanically, but to help international viewers understand the feeling behind the words.

## 🌏 How to Create a Translation

Everyone is welcome to use the Chinese SRT as a starting point for a translation into another language.

### Step 1: Download the Chinese SRT

Download the completed `.srt` file from this repository.

### Step 2: Open It in a Subtitle Editor

You can use a subtitle editor such as Subtitle Edit, Aegisub, or another tool that supports SRT files.

Make sure the file is saved in UTF-8 encoding so that Chinese characters and other languages display correctly.

### Step 3: Translate the Subtitles

Translate the Chinese dialogue into your language while preserving:

- Subtitle sequence numbers.
- Original timestamps.
- The SRT file structure.
- The meaning, tone, and personality of the speakers.

Please pay special attention to Chinese cultural references, food names, puns, and internet expressions.

You may adapt a phrase to make it sound natural in your language, but try to preserve the original meaning and humor.

### Step 4: Keep Cultural Notes Understandable

The Chinese subtitle file may include supplementary explanations about Chinese culture, music, food, or language.

These notes are included to help international viewers understand the program more deeply.

When translating them, please distinguish explanatory notes from the original dialogue whenever possible. You may adapt the wording to suit your audience, provided the original meaning is preserved.

### Step 5: Save and Share Your Translation

Save your translated file as an SRT file using a clear filename, for example:

-（ will update soon）

Please check the timing, spelling, punctuation, and line breaks before sharing your completed translation.

If you would like to contribute your translation to this repository, feel free to open an issue or submit a pull request.
